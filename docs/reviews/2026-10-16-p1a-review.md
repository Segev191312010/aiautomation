## Findings (defects first)

### F-1 MEDIUM: the T-2 pair budget delays a real Gateway outage page by up to 30 min, and hides that outage's child exits meanwhile

**Where:**
- `trading/operations/hysteresis.py:254-262`: `_announce_if_allowed` allows at most 1 announced OPEN per 30 min.
- `trading/operations/notify_service.py:937-952`: `broker_exit` folds exits whenever `hysteresis.is_open`, including an open the budget is holding back.

**Probe:** `probes/test_zz_review_old_blip.py`, the same timeline run on both trees.
- A first sweep at 08:10.
- A 70-second blip (3 failed connects, 08:15:00–08:16:10), with sweeps from 08:17.
- The Gateway dies for real at 08:25 and stays down until 09:11, with a screener failure every 70 s.

| | 1120b75 (live candidate) | 299d6e1 |
|---|---|---|
| blip | 08:15:00 alert, 08:17:00 reconnected | 08:16:10 alert, 08:19:00 reconnected |
| real outage page | **08:25:00** | **08:46:10** (silence 0:21:10) |
| repeat / recovery | 08:55 repeat, 09:11 reconnected | 09:13 reconnected |

The hysteresis did not suppress the blip, because three failures meet the count rule. It did delay the real outage. Before 09:25 nothing else reaches the phone: the bridge's own CRITICAL lines only start once the bridge runs. So a Gateway that dies between about 08:30 and 09:25, shortly after an announced blip, can stay silent until after the open. Intra-session, the bridge's CRITICAL "TWS did not answer" lines still go out, so the phone is not fully silent there.

The module docstring states the trade, and Appendix C approved "≤ 1 pair per 30 minutes per cause". The cost is that sustained outages are delayed, not only flaps.

**Required fix:**
- The budget may hold back an open only while its failure streak is younger than a sustain limit, for example 5 min of failures with no success.
- An open that has failed continuously that long is announced regardless of the budget. Flaps by definition never last that long, so the 70-s alternation test (one pair plus a flap count) still holds.
- Do not fold child exits while the open is held back (or announce first).
- Add the probe above as a test. It fails today.

### F-3 LOW: the 2 s port probe is synchronous inside the supervisor's event loop; with suspended children and a hung port, rechecks stack up

**Where:**
- `supervisor.py:278-284`: `socket.create_connection(..., timeout=2.0)`, called from `_count_exit` at line 863 for each restartable exit.
- `supervisor.py:935`: `_review_ledger` re-checks once per suspended child every `PORT_RECHECK` (15 s) while its last exit found the port not open.
- One loop iteration can therefore run (#exits + #suspended children due) probes back to back.

**What blocks:** for each probe, the entire supervisor loop blocks. That covers reaping, every start (the bridge's included), window-end SIGTERM/SIGKILL and signal handling. The children themselves keep running, and their stdout goes to files, not pipes.

**When it happens:**
- A refused connect on loopback returns at once, so the normal "Gateway logged out" case costs microseconds.
- The 2-s case needs connects that time out. One example is a frozen Gateway whose listen backlog has filled; the probes' own connect-and-close cycles fill it.

**Probe** `test_simultaneous_exits_probe_serially_and_suspended_rechecks_stack`: each probe charged 2 s of wall time; five strategies suspended; the port then timing out. 409 probes in 20 min, i.e. **818 s of 1200 s blocked (68%)**.

**Impact:**
- In the worst case a batch is about 2 s × 9 ≈ 18 s.
- The stop path stops probing once it sees the stop (ledgers reset at line 766). So shutdown fits launchd's `ExitTimeOut` 45 s: at most ~18 s of probes plus the 20 s grace.
- Bridge restarts and window-end terminations can lag by one batch.
- No lost session, no order impact.

**Fix:** one probe per loop iteration shared by all children (or a result cached for ≥ `PORT_RECHECK`), or probe off the loop (`asyncio.open_connection` + `wait_for`, or `to_thread`).
